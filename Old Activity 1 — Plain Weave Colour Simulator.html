Observation Journal

1 What is the optical mix colour you see when looking at the weave from a distance?
2 Does the warp or weft colour appear to dominate? Why might this be?
3 Try a complementary pair (colours opposite on the colour wheel). What happens at the thread junctions?
4 Now try two very similar colours (analogous pair). How does the perceived cloth colour differ from either thread?

What to look for:
  • Vibration: Complementary pairs seem to shimmer or pulse at the thread junctions
  • Advancing/receding: Warm colours visually advance; cool colours recede
  • Value shift: Dark warp on light weft makes the cloth appear as a medium tone
  • Hue shift: The perceived mix colour often shifts toward neither original hue
  • Disappearing contrast: Very similar colours merge into a near-solid surface
In plain weave, simultaneous contrast is at its most subtle — threads are so closely interlaced that optical mixing dominates. As you move to longer floats (4-shaft and 8-shaft structures), the colour areas grow larger and true simultaneous contrast begins to appear.
