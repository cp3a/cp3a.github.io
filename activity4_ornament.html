Observation Journal

1 Start with the original scheme (black/cyan warp, mixed weft). Describe the pattern. Which colour appears dominant and why?
2 Switch to the rust/amber warp preset. How does the change in warp colour affect the character of the ornament motif?
3 Change the weft scheme to a single solid colour. What changes compared to the original mixed weft sequence?
4 Edit the tieup — change just one cell. Describe what new pattern emerges in the drawdown.
5 Try switching to the Point draw or Rosepath threading. How does a different threading change the pattern even with the same tieup and colours?
6 Design your own colour combination. What relationship between warp colour, weft colour and tieup makes the strongest visual effect?
Suggested experiments:
What to look for:
  • Warp/weft ratio: Where warp floats are longer the warp colour dominates locally, creating simultaneous contrast with weft-dominated areas
  • Border threads: Threads 1, 12, 13, and 24 are on shaft 1 — they form the frame between repeats. Colouring them differently from the interior changes whether the pattern reads as outlined or filled
  • Tieup symmetry: A straight-draw tieup produces a plain-weave texture; combining shafts on one treadle creates longer floats and richer patterns
  • Weft accent row: The blue pick (row 6) in the original marks the centre of the repeat — this is where simultaneous contrast between warp and weft is strongest
  • Value contrast: Try a weft much lighter or darker than the warp — the ornament motif becomes more or less legible
The threading has an intentional symmetry: two 12-thread repeats, each with border threads (on shaft 1) framing a returning interior draw. Border threads are at positions 1, 12, 13 and 24. Editing the tieup affects border and interior threads differently, giving independent control over the pattern's outline vs. its field colour.
