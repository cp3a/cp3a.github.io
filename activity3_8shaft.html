Colour experiments to try:
What to look for:
  • Block edge: This is where simultaneous contrast is most intense — each block modifies the perceived colour of its neighbour
  • Threshold: Below ~25% contrast, blocks begin to merge. This proves that block weaves need contrast to function visually
  • Float length effect: Longer floats (satin vs. S&W) = purer colour areas = stronger simultaneous contrast per block
  • 4-colour interaction: When 2 warp + 2 weft colours cross each block, the blocks interact across all four colour relationships
The Josef Albers principle: In Summer & Winter, Block A and Block B each contain a large area of one colour. These large areas influence each other across the boundary — Block A makes Block B's colour appear to shift in hue, and vice versa. This is true simultaneous contrast, not optical mixing.
