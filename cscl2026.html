Dr. Bodong Chen

Associate Professor, University of Pennsylvania Graduate School of Education  ·  Homepage

Bodong Chen is an Associate Professor of Learning Sciences and Technologies at the University of Pennsylvania Graduate School of Education, where he directs the Penn GSE Wonder Lab and co-directs the Living Laboratory for Human Agency and AI.

His CSCL research looks at how knowledge-building communities take collective responsibility for advancing ideas, and how technology, analytics, and AI can support that work. Current lines of work include AI as epistemic infrastructure, network and temporal analysis of collaborative discourse, and infrastructuring for sustained CSCL innovation.

Dr. Chen joined the CSCL community as a doctoral student, presenting at CSCL 2011 in Hong Kong. Since then, he has presented regularly at CSCL and ICLS and has organized pre-conference workshops and symposia on emerging topics in the field. In ISLS, he served as Program Co-Chair of CSCL 2022 and 2025, an elected member of the CSCL Committee (2018–2022), and Co-Chair of the Membership Committee (2017–2020). He is an Associate Editor of ijCSCL and an editorial board member of JLS.

Dr. Mia Čarapina

Professor of Professional Studies in Computer Science, Zagreb University of Applied Sciences, Croatia  ·  Homepage

Mia Čarapina is a Professor of Professional Studies in Computer Science at Zagreb University of Applied Sciences (TVZ), Croatia. Her research focuses on computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL), mobile learning, and human-computer interaction (HCI), with a particular emphasis on primary education. Her broader research interests include developing computational thinking, educational robotics, game-based learning, artificial intelligence in CSCL, and future of education. Her doctoral research investigated how shared tablets can support individual and collaborative learning, focusing on how multiple primary school students interact and collaborate using a single device.

Dr. Čarapina is Editor-in-Chief of the Springer book series CHAPTERS in Education and a member of the Editorial Board of the International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (ijCSCL). She is actively involved in the international CSCL community through research collaborations, particularly within the CONNECT network, which she co-founded, as well as through her membership in the ISLS CSCL Junior Subcommittee, conference participation, and community service. She participated in the CSCL 2015 conference and the ISLS Annual Meeting 2025. She is a regular reviewer for several international journals and conferences, including the ijCSCL, Learning, Media and Technology, PLOS ONE, the ISLS Annual Meeting, IEEE ICALT, and AIED.

She contributes to international research initiatives and the organization of academic events, including the Postdigital Backlash conference in Zagreb (2026) and the 2027 EATEL Summer School on Technology Enhanced Learning (JTELSS) in Zadar, Croatia, where she serves as a local chair. She is particularly interested in strengthening connections within the CSCL community, supporting early-career researchers, and fostering international collaboration.

Dr. Gahgene Gweon

Professor, Seoul National University  ·  Homepage

Gahgene Gweon is a Professor at Seoul National University, where she directs the Cognitive Computing Lab in the Graduate School of Convergence Science and Technology. Her research sits at the intersection of human-computer interaction, learning sciences, artificial intelligence, and natural language processing. Broadly, she is interested in how computing systems can help us model human cognitive processes and better understand how people learn, communicate, and collaborate.

Dr. Gweon's connection to the CSCL community has developed through research on collaborative problem solving, dialogue-based learning support, conversational agents, and the analysis of student interaction. She has presented work at CSCL and ICLS, reviewed for the community over the years, served on program committees, and mentored students whose work engages with CSCL questions and methods. Serving as Program Chair for CSCL 2019 and CSCL 2026 gave her a close view of the intellectual breadth of the community and the importance of supporting participation across regions, career stages, and methodological traditions. She also served on the ISLS Publication Committee from January 2019 to July 2026.

Across these roles, her work has focused on making learners' thinking and group processes more visible, and on exploring how AI can support human collaboration. If elected to the CSCL Committee, her hope is to contribute to strengthening international participation, supporting emerging scholars, and helping the community think together about the future of CSCL in an era of AI-mediated learning and collaboration.

Dr. Xiao Hu

Associate Professor, College of Information Science, University of Arizona  ·  Homepage

Xiao Hu is an Associate Professor in the College of Information Science at the University of Arizona. Her research integrates CSCL, learning analytics, and AI in education, supporting collaborative writing, maker activities, and problem solving using novel technology. She has been recognized among the world's top 2% scientists in the Stanford citation-based rankings, obtained external grants amounting to US$8M, and created seven platforms used by 5,000+ learners.

Dr. Hu has published in ijCSCL and ISLS/CSCL since 2021. She was invited as a speaker at an ISLS'24 workshop on CSCL community-building and co-organized two CSCL workshops at ISLS'26. Following the 2025 CSCL paradigm-building workshop, she continued co-authoring an ijCSCL article on redefining the scope of CSCL.

Dr. Hu has served on program committees for ISLS/CSCL since 2020, LAK since 2016, and AIED since 2024, including senior roles. She serves on the editorial board of the British Journal of Educational Technology (BJET) and is co-editing a special collection on generative AI in CSCL. She has served as an ICALT track co-chair since 2017 and as Vice Chair for Community Engagement of the IEEE Computer Society Technical Community on Learning Technology since 2025. If elected, Dr. Hu's interdisciplinary background would position her well to strengthen connections between CSCL and the learning analytics, AI in education, and learning technology communities.

Dr. Heisawn Jeong

Professor of Psychology, Hallym University, South Korea  ·  Homepage

Heisawn Jeong is Professor of Psychology at Hallym University, South Korea. Her research lies at the intersection of cognitive psychology, the learning sciences, and computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL). Her CSCL research has focused on collaborative knowledge construction, the role of interaction in learning, the affordances of digital technologies for supporting collaboration, and the relationships among theory, methods, and empirical research in CSCL. More recently, she has become increasingly interested in artificial intelligence, particularly generative AI, and its implications for learning, teaching, and higher education.

Dr. Jeong has been substantially involved in the international Learning Sciences community and was a regular participant in CSCL and ICLS conferences for many years. She served on the International Society of the Learning Sciences (ISLS) Board of Directors from 2014 to 2021 and as ISLS President in 2019–2020, and was inducted as an ISLS Fellow in 2021. She has also contributed to the community as a mentor for doctoral consortia and mid-career workshops. She has served on the editorial board of the International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning and currently serves as an Associate Editor of Instructional Science. In addition, she has contributed to professional service in Korea through organizations such as the Korean Educational Psychology Association and the Korean Psychological Association.

Dr. Khor Ean Teng

Lecturer/Education Research Scientist, National Institute of Education, NTU Singapore  ·  Homepage

Dr. Khor Ean Teng is a Lecturer/Education Research Scientist at the National Institute of Education (NIE), Nanyang Technological University (NTU), Singapore. With a background in computer science and educational technology, her work bridges technology and education, with research interests spanning Human-AI Collaboration, AI in Education, learning analytics, educational data mining, and technology-enhanced learning. She is particularly interested in how AI can become an active partner in learning and how human–AI interactions can be designed to support meaningful, personalised, and collaborative learning.

Her connection to the CSCL community reflects her growing interest in how the notion of collaboration evolves when AI becomes part of the learning environment. Her recent work examines learners' interactions with generative AI and educational chatbots, including how students engage with AI-generated feedback and how individual and contextual factors shape these interactions. She is interested in extending established perspectives on socially situated and technology-mediated learning to consider the roles of AI, learner agency, and human–AI collaboration in emerging learning environments.

Dr. Khor has participated in CSCL 2026, CSCL 2024, and ICLS 2024. She has also contributed to the ISLS community as a Programme Committee member for ISLS 2024, 2025, and 2026, and served as a mentor for the ISLS Doctoral Consortium in 2024. She is also the founding leader of the Learning Analytics Special Interest Group at NIE, further connecting her interests in learning, data, and technology.

Dr. Elizabeth Koh

Senior Education Research Scientist (Senior Lecturer), National Institute of Education, NTU Singapore  ·  Homepage

Elizabeth Koh is a Senior Education Research Scientist (Senior Lecturer) at the Centre for Research in Pedagogy and Practice, National Institute of Education, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore. Her research focuses on computer-supported collaborative learning, learning analytics, lifelong learning, and educational technology innovations. She has attended eight ICLS, CSCL, and ISLS Annual Meetings: ICLS 2014, ICLS 2016, CSCL 2017, ICLS 2018, CSCL 2019, ICLS 2020, ISLS 2022, and ISLS 2023, and intends to attend ISLS 2027.

Since 2016, Dr. Koh has demonstrated sustained international leadership and service across the International Society of the Learning Sciences (ISLS), the Asia-Pacific Society for Computers in Education (APSCE), and the Society for Learning Analytics Research (SoLAR). Within ISLS, she has served twice as Co-Chair of the CSCL programme at ISLS Annual Meetings (2023, 2027), provided editorial service to the International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning since 2020, and served as a juror for CSCL 2025.

Beyond ISLS, she has held a range of Chair, Co-Chair, and mentor roles across APSCE conference programmes and the APSCE Special Interest Group (SIG) on CSCL and Learning Sciences since 2018. She has also contributed to the SoLAR community as Posters Co-Chair for LAK 2022 and through invited engagements. Since 2014, she has served as Associate Editor and subsequently Co-Editor of Learning: Research and Practice, supporting the dissemination of research in CSCL, learning sciences, and educational technology.

Dr. Zhichun (Lukas) Liu

Assistant Professor, Faculty of Education, University of Hong Kong  ·  Homepage

Zhichun (Lukas) Liu is an Assistant Professor of Learning Design and Implementation Sciences in the Faculty of Education at the University of Hong Kong. He earned his Ph.D. from Florida State University and previously served as a Visiting Assistant Professor at Teachers College, Columbia University, and a Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Massachusetts Dartmouth.

Dr. Liu's CSCL research focuses on enabling human agency and collaborative problem-solving within complex learning environments. He designs immersive, active game-based learning experiences that empower learners in increasingly networked and technologically mediated spaces. He leverages these environments as situated contexts for advanced learning, challenging students to critically navigate multifaceted sociotechnical, environmental, and civic challenges. Methodologically, his work adopts approaches like stealth assessment and Quantitative Ethnography to blend quantitative analytics with qualitative inquiry to dynamically model learners' behaviors, collaborative discourse, and cognitive interactions without disrupting the learning process. Notably, his work has been nominated for the best papers at conferences, including ICLS and ICQE.

He is a dedicated member of the ISLS community, regularly publishing and presenting his work at ICLS and CSCL conferences. His service to the community includes acting as a conference reviewer for ISLS since 2018 and serving as a Committee Member on the ISLS Communications Committee in 2024. His broader service includes leadership roles such as past President of the AECT Division of Emerging Learning Technologies and Chair of the APSCE SIG on Educational Gamification and Game-based Learning.

Dr. Ceren Ocak

Assistant Professor of Instructional Technology, Georgia Southern University  ·  Homepage

Ceren Ocak is an Assistant Professor of Instructional Technology at Georgia Southern University. Her research interests are the embodiment of computational thinking, multimodal interaction analysis, and computer-supported collaborative learning.

Dr. Ocak has spent the last decade exploring young children's computational thinking as an embodied phenomenon, beginning with video analysis of fifth-grade learners working together to program a robot. That study became her first publication at CSCL 2019, where her connection to the community began. She vividly remembers how positive that experience was for her as an early doctoral student — she felt that she belonged, and she would like to contribute to that tradition by welcoming others as she was once welcomed.

Since then, she has stayed connected to the community by publishing in the ISLS proceedings (2023, 2025, and 2026) and serving as a reviewer and program committee member since 2022. She has taken on similar roles in communities closely connected to her research on embodied learning (e.g., ICCE International Workshop on Embodied Learning). She has also reviewed work for flagship conferences (AERA, AECT), reviewed manuscripts, and edited special issues for journals in education and educational technology (e.g., ETR&D). Her reviewing, editing, and program committee work across these communities, particularly ICLS, has shown her how a community grows and thrives by supporting new members and connecting researchers who aim to advance collaborative learning and technology.

Dr. Jennifer Olsen

Associate Professor, University of San Diego  ·  Homepage

Jennifer K. Olsen is an Associate Professor at the University of San Diego within the Computer Science Department. She received her PhD and MS from Carnegie Mellon University in Human-Computer Interaction and her BS in Cognitive Science. Before joining USD, Jenny was a postdoctoral researcher at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology, Lausanne, Switzerland (EPFL). Her work focuses on the development of educational technologies that support students engaging in collaborative learning at the undergraduate level, developing technology support for instructors to facilitate these learning processes, and using multi-modal data gathered from these interactions to better understand the collaborative learning process. Her projects currently focus on collaborative learning within the domain of computer science.

Jenny attended her first CSCL conference in 2015 in Gothenburg, Sweden and has been a member of ISLS since. She has been part of the ISLS communications committee since 2019 and a co-chair of the committee since 2022 with her co-chair term ending this year. She is an ICLS Conference Program Co-chair for the 2027 ISLS Annual Meeting and has joined the CSCL task forces led by the CSCL Community Committee. Jenny has been connected to the CSCL community since her graduate studies and would like to provide support for the interdisciplinary community to help it thrive.

Dr. Kylie Peppler

Professor of Informatics and Education, University of California, Irvine  ·  Homepage

Kylie Peppler is a Professor of Informatics and Education at the University of California, Irvine (UCI), where she directs the Creativity Labs and is affiliated with the Connected Learning Lab. Her research examines how people learn, create, and collaborate through emerging technologies, with particular interests in collaborative learning, creativity, making, and the design of new learning environments. Her current work explores human-AI collaboration, AI literacy, computational creativity, and AI-enabled tools for learning and creative expression across informal settings.

Dr. Peppler has been an active member of the ISLS community since 2006, attending ISLS conferences regularly and presenting her work at both ICLS and CSCL. She currently serves on the Editorial Board of the International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (ijCSCL). She has also contributed to ISLS through extensive conference participation, reviewing, and service. She most recently served as the chair of the local organizing committee for ISLS 2026 at UCI and currently sits on the ISLS Annual Meeting Committee (AMC).

Dr. Manoli Pifarré

Full Professor of Educational Psychology, Universitat de Lleida, Spain  ·  Homepage

Manoli Pifarré is Full Professor of Educational Psychology at the Universitat de Lleida (Spain), where she leads the research group Context and Cognition mediated by Technologies (COnTIC). Her research focuses on the dialogical use of technology as a tool to foster distinctive cognitive, metacognitive, and social processes in collaborative learning. She has led eight consecutive national R&D projects funded by Spanish ministries and has coordinated her university's team in three European research projects. Her work spans learning design, CSCL, collaborative learning analytics, and the educational use of artificial intelligence. She is currently investigating how Viacocrea, a platform she co-designed, can orchestrate, enrich, and support collaboration and creativity in classrooms.

Her ties to the international CSCL community have grown through collaborative research stays abroad. She was an Honorary Research Fellow at the University of Exeter (UK) and is an associate research member of the Cambridge Educational Dialogue Research Group (CEDIR) at the University of Cambridge. A long-standing member of ISLS, she regularly takes part in its conferences and activities.

If elected, Dr. Pifarré hopes to contribute to the committee in three ways: by bridging CSCL research and classroom practice, drawing on her experience designing and testing technology with teachers and students; by strengthening the connection between CSCL and educational dialogue research; and by promoting the responsible, pedagogically grounded integration of AI in collaborative learning.

Dr. Sebastian Strauß

Postdoctoral Researcher, Center for Advanced Internet Studies & Ruhr University Bochum, Germany  ·  Homepage

Sebastian Strauß is a postdoctoral researcher at the Center for Advanced Internet Studies and at Ruhr University Bochum, Germany. With his background in educational psychology, he is researching the challenges that learners face during collaborative learning in small groups and how different forms of support can help groups overcome these.

During his doctoral studies, Dr. Strauß examined typical challenges for groups and investigated the effects of adaptive prompts and group awareness tools on collaboration. In his postdoctoral research, he has broadened this focus to regulation processes and the development of collaboration skills. Recently, he began exploring GenAI in CSCL contexts to contribute to the voice of the community in shaping the discourse on how this technology can, and should (not), be integrated into educational settings.

He has been an ISLS member since 2018, first attending the CSCL conference in 2019. Since then, he has participated in ISLS Annual Meetings with contributions in different formats and served as a (meta-)reviewer for the conference and the ijCSCL. During the pandemic in 2021, he was part of the core local organizing team for the ISLS Annual Meeting in Bochum. At the 2023 ISLS Annual Meeting in Montréal, a paper he co-authored with colleagues from Bochum was nominated for Best Paper Award. Through these experiences, he has developed a strong connection to the community and would welcome the chance to contribute his perspective and expertise to the CSCL Committee.
